# School Management Software Evaluation Checklist

Choosing school management software affects admissions, student records, academics, finance, attendance and parent communication. Before you compare vendors or book a demo, involve the people who manage those workflows every day.

Use this checklist to identify what your school needs, who should take part in the evaluation and the questions worth asking before you make a decision.

**CTA: Discuss Your School’s Requirements**

---

## Start with the school problems you need to solve

Do not begin with a long feature list. Start with the work that is difficult to manage today. A useful school management system should fit the way information moves between admissions, administration, academics, accounts and parents.

Before reviewing software, write down:

- Which processes still depend on separate spreadsheets, paper files or disconnected tools?
- Which teams regularly need the same student, fee or academic information?
- Where does information need to move from one team to another without being entered again?
- Which reports or updates take too long to prepare?
- Which school or campus-level decisions need clearer visibility?

This gives every stakeholder a shared starting point before comparing products.

---

## Bring the right stakeholders into the evaluation

A school-management decision should not sit with one person alone. The people responsible for daily operations can identify requirements that a generic product demonstration may not reveal.

| Stakeholder | Questions they should help answer |
|---|---|
| School leadership | Which operational, academic and financial views are needed to run the school? |
| Admissions and front desk | How are enquiries, registrations, counselling, follow-up and enrolment handled today? |
| Academic coordinators and teachers | How are timetables, examinations, report cards, assessments and student progress managed? |
| Accounts team | How are fee heads, concessions, collections, receipts, dues and finance reports managed? |
| Administration and HR | How are attendance exceptions, staff processes, role access and day-to-day operations managed? |
| IT or data owner | Which records must be prepared, imported, checked and protected during setup? |
| Campus leadership | What needs central visibility, and what should remain with each branch? |

## 1. Check the core workflows your school needs

Use the following questions to identify whether a prospective platform supports the workflows that matter to your school. Do not treat every capability as mandatory; prioritise the modules your teams actually need.

### Admissions and enrolment

- Can the admissions team record walk-in enquiries and registrations in one workflow?
- Can the school organise prospective-student information and see where each enquiry is in its defined process?
- Can accepted applicants become enrolled student records without re-entering the same core information?
- Can the school define appropriate follow-up responsibility and use an official school communication process where needed?

### Student information and daily administration

- Can authorised teams work from a connected student record rather than separate files?
- Does the school need student information connected with enrolment, attendance, academics, fees, report cards and parent access?
- Which records are held today, and which fields must remain accurate after setup?
- If the school has multiple branches, which master data and directories need central oversight?

### Academics and examinations

- Can academic teams manage the timetable, teacher allocation, sections, periods, breaks and subject requirements?
- How will the school handle timetable clashes and substitute-teacher arrangements?
- Which examination workflows are required, such as seating plans, invigilator schedules, hall tickets and report cards?
- Which teams need access to academic progress or report-card information?

### Fees and finance

- Which fee heads does the school need to configure, such as tuition, admission, transport, sports, term or lab fees?
- Which payment methods and receipt processes are used today?
- How are concessions, outstanding dues, collections and finance reports reviewed?
- Which fee information should be visible to accounts staff, leadership, parents and students?

### Attendance, HR and operations

- How is student and staff attendance recorded today?
- Does the school use supported networked biometric or RFID attendance devices, and how are exceptions reviewed?
- Which staff attendance, leave, payroll and salary-slip workflows need to be connected?
- What operational work—such as inventory, ID cards or school events—needs a more structured process?

### Parent and student communication

- Which information should parents and students be able to view, such as fee status, report cards or school updates?
- Which communication should be sent through an official school-owned channel?
- Who should be authorised to create, review and send parent communications?

## 2. Evaluate how information moves between teams

The value of school ERP software is not only in individual modules. It is in the way relevant school information can move between authorised teams.

Use these questions during a product evaluation:

1. Can an accepted admission enquiry become an enrolled student record without duplicate data entry?
2. Can accounts apply the relevant fee structure and record payment information against the correct student?
3. Can academic and administrative teams use the information they need for attendance, timetables, examinations and report cards?
4. Can parents and students access the information the school chooses to share?
5. Can leadership review the appropriate academic, operational and financial information without replacing departmental workflows?

Ask the vendor to demonstrate one realistic end-to-end school journey rather than isolated feature screens.

---

## 3. Review user access and branch structure

Every user should not see or change the same information. Before selecting a platform, define who needs access and what each role should be able to manage.

| Area to define | Questions to ask |
|---|---|
| Roles | Which people need administrative, admissions, accounts, HR, teacher, parent or student access? |
| Responsibilities | Which tasks should each team be able to view, create, update or approve? |
| Campuses | Does the school need separate campus workflows as well as central visibility? |
| Data ownership | Who owns the accuracy of student, staff, enquiry and finance records? |
| Ongoing review | Who will review access when staff responsibilities change? |

For multi-campus schools, ask how central teams manage institution-wide master data while campus teams work with their own day-to-day operations.

---

## 4. Prepare records before discussing imports

Software cannot make inconsistent source data reliable on its own. Before moving records from spreadsheets or existing systems, decide what information should be imported and who will validate it.

### Record-preparation checklist

- Identify the student, staff and enquiry records that need to be moved.
- Agree the required fields for each record type.
- Remove or resolve duplicate entries before import.
- Check that names, contact details, campus and class information follow a consistent format.
- Decide who will provide the source files and who will review the imported information.
- Keep an approved source copy and a clear record of corrections made during preparation.

Ask the vendor exactly which import formats and record types are supported. Do not assume that a product will automatically clean, migrate or validate data unless that scope is confirmed in writing.

---

## 5. Use the demo to test your real workflows

A demo should help the school answer whether the system suits its operating model. Share your priorities in advance so the discussion can focus on relevant workflows.

Bring examples such as:

- An admissions journey from first enquiry through enrolment
- A current fee structure, concession policy or collection-report requirement
- A timetable with teacher availability, sections, periods, breaks and subject weightage
- A report-card or examination process the academic team needs to manage
- A list of data records the school expects to prepare for import
- The branch structure and teams that need role-appropriate access

### India-specific evaluation questions

For schools in India, ask vendors to demonstrate your board-specific examination and report-card formats, academic-year structure, fee instalments and concessions. Check how cash, UPI, cheque and bank-transfer payments are recorded and reconciled, which languages parents need, and whether existing biometric or RFID devices are compatible. Distinguish payment recording from an integrated online payment gateway.

### Questions to ask in the demo

1. Which modules fit our current priorities, and which can wait?
2. How will the platform support the way our admissions, academic, finance and administrative teams work?
3. What records can be imported, and what preparation is required from our school?
4. How are role access and multi-campus workflows configured?
5. What responsibilities remain with our school during setup and validation?
6. Which claims in this demo can the vendor document in the commercial scope or product specification?

---

## 6. Check data protection, costs and ongoing support

These questions apply to every vendor. They help the school compare documented responsibilities and commercial scope; they are not claims that any particular provider offers a certification, service level or integration.

- Who can access student, parent, staff and financial information, including vendor personnel?
- What backup and recovery arrangements are documented, and who is responsible for restoring records?
- Can the school export its records in usable formats? What happens to access and stored data when the contract ends?
- Which charges cover setup, imports, training, support, additional campuses, communication usage and renewal?
- What support channels, service hours and escalation arrangements are included?
- What onboarding or training is provided, and what must school staff complete themselves?

Ask for the answers in the proposal, agreement or vendor documentation where appropriate. Keep important requirements open until the school has sufficient written evidence.

## 7. Compare vendors consistently

Use the same criteria for every product you evaluate. A consistent scorecard makes it easier to distinguish a useful workflow fit from a polished generic demonstration.

Mark each requirement as **Must-have**, **Useful** or **Future**. Record its status as **Demonstrated**, **Documented**, **Unconfirmed** or **Not available**. Keep any unanswered must-have requirement open until the vendor provides sufficient evidence.

## FAQs

### Who should use a school management software evaluation checklist?

School leadership, admissions, academic, finance, administration and data owners should use it together. Each team can identify the workflows, records and permissions that matter to its daily work before the school compares products.

### What should we prepare before a school software demo?

Prepare the workflows you want to improve, the teams involved, examples of admissions, academic or finance requirements, the records you may need to import and your campus structure. This helps the discussion focus on your school’s actual requirements.

### Should every school choose the same modules?

No. Schools should select the modules that match their operational requirements. Begin with the workflows your teams need now, then document any future requirements separately.

### What should we ask about data imports?

Ask which record types and fields are supported, how source files should be prepared, who checks the imported information and what responsibilities remain with the school. Do not assume data will be cleaned or migrated automatically unless the scope is confirmed.

### How can a multi-campus school evaluate software?

Define which records and reporting need central visibility, what each campus manages locally, which roles need access and how master data should be maintained. Ask vendors to demonstrate the central and campus workflow rather than describing it only in broad terms.

### Does this checklist recommend a specific school management system?

No. The checklist is designed to help a school compare software against its own requirements. Cleezo Class can demonstrate relevant workflows in a demo, but the school should evaluate the documented product scope and commercial proposal before making a decision.
